## Your current brand colours

Your identity is a **green system**, which is genuinely good news. It's distinctive (most creative studios default to black/white or the generic purple-blue you saw on Marshall Haber), it reads as growth/performance-oriented which fits your DTC pivot perfectly, and it gives you a real ownable hue. The colours I pulled:

**Deep forest green** `#073624` — your darkest, the hero background
**Rich teal-green** `#0E2E2D` / `#0A2629` — the dark bands and footer
**Bright emerald** `#1DBF73` — your accent / highlight / CTA green
**Off-white** `#F4F6F5` — backgrounds and light sections
**White** `#FFFFFF` — text on dark, clean panels

So your real palette is essentially: **two darks (forest + teal), one bright green accent, and off-white/white.** That's a tight, confident palette, which as I mentioned last message is actually *better* than a many-colour scheme for looking premium.

## My honest recommendation: keep the green, drop the rainbow

Here's the key decision. Marshall Haber's design works by giving each service block a *different* loud colour (purple, orange, blue, black). If you copy that structure but force your green identity into it, you have two bad options: either you go rainbow and lose your green identity, or you make eight green blocks and it gets monotonous.

The better move, and what'll actually look more premium: **don't do eight different colours at all.** Use your green system as the spine and let the *work imagery* provide the colour variety. This is more sophisticated than the rainbow approach and it keeps you unmistakably "Scaler green." A focused palette used confidently beats a rainbow every time, especially for the Superside-tier positioning you want.

So I'm going to map the sections using only your real colours plus tonal variation, not invented hues.
